1        Introduction
1.1      Overview
E-Bank gives a bank one platform for onboarding customers, running their accounts, cards and loans, and keeping
every operation compliant, so the bank runs one coherent system instead of several products that were never
designed to work together. The buyer is the bank. The users are its customers (individuals and small companies, called
legal entities), who work in a mobile and web app, and its employees (support, compliance and AML officers, loan
officers, accountants, administrators), who work in a back-office console.
The platform consists of eight modules that talk to each other through synchronous APIs (application programming
interfaces) and a shared event bus: core banking (CBS) with the payment system, KYC (know your customer), CRM
(customer relationship management), card management, loans, accounting, AML (anti-money laundering) and
notifications.

1.2      The problem
A bank usually runs customer data, accounts, cards, payments, compliance and accounting on separate systems that
were bought or built at different times by different teams. They were not designed to work together, so integrating
them is slow and error-prone, and every change risks breaking something else.

registry, SEPA and SWIFT networks, card network, FNTT and the notification providers are described as interfaces and
can be mocks during implementation.

3         System architecture
3.1       Context




                          System context: modules, synchronous calls, event bus and external systems

The platform has eight modules: KYC (know your customer), CRM (customer relationship management), CBS (core
banking system) and Payment, Cards, Loans, Accounting, AML (anti-money laundering) and Notification. Customers
and employees reach them through the API (application programming interface) gateway. External systems are the
IDV (identity verification) provider, the company registry, the SEPA (Single Euro Payments Area) and SWIFT (Society for
Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication) networks, the card network, FNTT (the Lithuanian financial
intelligence unit) and the push, e-mail and SMS (short message service) providers.
Synchronous calls between modules: Cards and CBS call AML to screen a payment; Cards and Loans call CBS to
reserve, post or check funds; Cards calls Loans for a credit-card assessment and CRM for a customer status when it has
no replica; CBS calls CRM to check the password at sign-in; Accounting pulls the day’s transactions from CBS and the
day’s completed credit-card purchases from Cards; CRM reads accounts and products from CBS, Cards and Loans
(read-only).

3.2       Communication rules
Synchronous calls (REST, Representational State Transfer, with JSON, JavaScript Object Notation) are used only when
the caller must wait for an answer, as listed in section 3.1. Customer and employee calls go through the API gateway
under /api/v1; calls between services use /internal/<module>.
Events are used for everything else. A module saves its change and the event in one database transaction
(transactional outbox) and the relay publishes it at least once. Consumers ignore an event whose eventId they have
already processed. Events about one customer are keyed by customerId, so their order is kept.
Independence: every module owns its database; no module reads another module’s tables. Other modules keep only
ids and small replicas fed by events (for example the customer status in CBS and Cards).
Envelope of every event: eventId (UUID), eventType, occurredAt (ISO 8601 UTC), source, payload. Event names use
UPPER_SNAKE_CASE; schemas change only by adding fields.
Errors of every API use the body {"error": {"code": "...", "message": "..."}}, for client errors 400, 401, 403, 404, 409 and
422 and for server errors 500, 502, 503 and 504. A caller treats a 5xx response, or no answer within its timeout, as a
temporary failure. It retries only idempotent calls, meaning GET or calls with an Idempotency-Key or another unique
id, with backoff, and then follows the fallback of the flow. For AML screening the fallback is section 11.3.1, A4 for
payments and A6 for cards.

Conventions: identifiers are UUID (universally unique identifier); money is Decimal(18,2), in the API a string with two
decimals; times are ISO 8601 (the international date and time format) in UTC (Coordinated Universal Time); screens
show Europe/Vilnius time.

5         UI principles
5.1       Navigation
Customer app (mobile, same structure on the web): a bottom navigation bar with five items: Home (account list),
Payments (new transfer), Cards, Loans and More (profile, notification settings, pending approvals, mandates, saved
recipients, KYC (know your customer) status and support, sign out). The active item is filled. Back is always a “<” at the
top left; the app never uses the bottom bar for actions.
Back-office console (web): a left menu that shows only the items allowed for the employee role: KYC review,
Customers, Accounts, AML (anti-money laundering) queue, Loan review, General ledger and reports, Audit log.

6         KYC
6.1       Scope and ownership
Know Your Customer (KYC) is the module that decides whether the bank may trust the identity of a customer. It runs
verification cases, keeps the identity evidence, records the result of every check, calculates the customer risk level and
stores the review decisions. KYC is one deployable service with its own database and an encrypted document vault. It
is operated by compliance officers (employee role COMPLIANCE), because KYC data has stricter access and retention
rules than other customer data.

6.4        Business rules
6.4.1      General rules

 ID              Rule
 K-BR-01         Required documents. INDIVIDUAL: one identity document (PASSPORT, ID_CARD or RESIDENCE_PERMIT), one
                 SELFIE and one PROOF_OF_ADDRESS issued in the last 3 months. LEGAL_ENTITY: a REGISTRY_EXTRACT issued in
                 the last 3 months and a UBO_DECLARATION; every active representative must be an INDIVIDUAL customer
                 whose own onboarding case is APPROVED.
 K-BR-02         Files. PDF (Portable Document Format), JPG or PNG, at most 10 MB per file and 10 files per case. The file goes
                 to the encrypted vault; the database keeps storageRef and the SHA-256 hash. A stored file is never changed: a
                 new upload of the same type replaces the old document, which stays in the case with status REJECTED and
                 reason REPLACED.
 K-BR-03         IDV attempts. At most 3 IDV attempts per case. After the third FAIL the case goes to MANUAL_REVIEW.
 K-BR-04         Screening. KYC publishes one SCREENING_REQUESTED per screened party: the subject, every active
                 representative and every UBO. A HIT is never cleared automatically; only a compliance officer can clear it as a
                 false positive, with a comment.
 K-BR-05         Risk score. The score is the sum of the points of all matching active risk rules (6.4.2). Level: LOW 0–29, MEDIUM
                 30–59, HIGH 60 or more.
 K-BR-06         Automatic approval. Only when the level is LOW, every check is PASS and there is no uncleared HIT. In every
                 other case the case goes to MANUAL_REVIEW.
 K-BR-07         Four-eyes principle. A HIGH risk case needs two APPROVE decisions by two different officers (approvalOrder 1
                 and 2). The officer who gave approval 1 cannot give approval 2. A REJECT or REQUEST_INFO by either officer
                 cancels approval 1. The rule is enforced on the server.
 K-BR-08         Comments. REJECT, REQUEST_INFO and clearing a HIT need a comment of 1–1000 characters; for APPROVE the
                 comment is optional. A case with an uncleared sanctions HIT cannot be approved.
 K-BR-09         Review dates. nextReviewDate = approval date + 1 year (HIGH), 3 years (MEDIUM) or 5 years (LOW). The
                 periods are configuration values.
 K-BR-10         Periodic review. A daily job at 02:00 Europe/Vilnius time opens a PERIODIC_REVIEW case when the due date is
                 30 days away. The due date is the earlier of nextReviewDate and the expiry date of the identity document. The
                 current approval stays valid until the due date. A review case that is not APPROVED 30 days after the due date
                 becomes EXPIRED.
 K-BR-11         Information requests. The due date is 30 days after the request. KYC sends one reminder 7 days before the due
                 date. A case still in PENDING_CUSTOMER after the due date becomes EXPIRED.
 K-BR-12         Change review. SENSITIVE_DATA_CHANGED opens a CHANGE_REVIEW case; if a case is already open, the new
                 snapshot is attached to it instead. Required documents: name, personal code or date of birth changed → new
                 identity document; address changed → proof of address; company structure changed → new UBO declaration
                 and a new registry check.
 K-BR-13         Customer status. KYC never changes the customer status. It publishes its outcome and CRM applies it (7.5.4).
 K-BR-14         Visibility. A customer sees only the cases of their own INDIVIDUAL customer and of companies where they are
                 an active representative. A customer never sees the risk score, the screening details or the reason
                 SANCTIONS_MATCH.

6.5      Business flows
6.5.1    Flow 1: Onboarding of an individual
Actors: customer, CRM, KYC, IDV provider, AML, compliance officer. Trigger: CUSTOMER_REGISTERED with type
INDIVIDUAL. Result: KYC_APPROVED, after which CRM activates the customer.
  1. KYC consumes CUSTOMER_REGISTERED, opens a KycCase of type ONBOARDING in status DRAFT, stores the first
     SubjectSnapshot and publishes KYC_CASE_OPENED. Later CUSTOMER_PROFILE_UPDATED events add new
     snapshot versions.
  2. The customer opens More > Verification (K-W1), uploads an identity document and proof of address and takes a
     selfie (K-BR-01, K-BR-02).
  3. The customer presses Submit. KYC checks that all required documents exist, sets SUBMITTED and then
     CHECKS_IN_PROGRESS, and answers at once; the customer does not wait for the checks.
  4. KYC sends the identity document and the selfie to the IDV provider and stores the result as two
     VerificationCheck records (IDENTITY_DOC, LIVENESS).
  5. KYC publishes SCREENING_REQUESTED for the subject. AML answers with SCREENING_COMPLETED (CLEAR or
     HIT), stored as SANCTIONS and PEP checks.
  6. KYC calculates the risk score (6.4.2). A LOW case without a HIT is approved automatically (K-BR-06); every other
     case goes to MANUAL_REVIEW (Flow 3).
  7. KYC sets APPROVED, calculates nextReviewDate (K-BR-09) and publishes KYC_APPROVED. CRM, AML and
     Notification consume it. KYC’s part ends here.

Alternative paths
  •   1A Required document missing (step 3). KYC answers 422 DOCUMENTS_MISSING and the case stays DRAFT. In
      K-W1 the Submit button is disabled until the list is complete.
  •   1B IDV fails (step 4). The document gets status REJECTED with a reason (for example

      KYC_INFO_REQUESTED so that the customer is notified. After a new upload and Submit the flow continues at

  •   1C Sanctions or PEP hit (step 5). The case goes to MANUAL_REVIEW. A confirmed sanctions match ends in

  •   1D Provider or AML outage (steps 4–5). No answer within 30 s or a 5xx answer stores the check as ERROR and
      the call is retried after 1, 5 and 15 minutes and then every hour. After 24 hours without a result the case goes to

        MANUAL_REVIEW, where the officer decides with the evidence available. The customer is never blocked by the

  •     1E Customer does not answer. A case that stays in PENDING_CUSTOMER after the due date becomes EXPIRED

6.5.2      Flow 2: Onboarding of a legal entity
The flow is the same as Flow 1, with these differences. The trigger is CUSTOMER_REGISTERED with type
LEGAL_ENTITY, published when a company representative adds a company in CRM (7.5.2).
  1. The representative selects the company in the For field of K-W1 and uploads the registry extract and the UBO
     declaration.
  2. On Submit, KYC checks that every active representative in the latest snapshot has an APPROVED onboarding
     case of their own. If not, it answers 422 REPRESENTATIVE_NOT_VERIFIED and names the persons.
  3. KYC queries the company registry with the registration number and country. The REGISTRY check is PASS when
     the company is ACTIVE and the legal name matches. The UBO_MATCH check is PASS when every registry owner
     with more than 25 % is declared and each declared share differs from the registry by at most 1 percentage
     point.
  4. KYC publishes SCREENING_REQUESTED for the company, every UBO and every representative (K-BR-04).
  5. Scoring and decision as in Flow 1. A UBO_MATCH FAIL always goes to manual review; the officer may accept the
     declaration (RF-08) or request information.

6.5.3      Flow 3: Manual review and four-eyes approval
Actors: compliance officer (role COMPLIANCE), a second compliance officer for HIGH risk. Precondition: the case is in
MANUAL_REVIEW or AWAITING_SECOND_APPROVAL.
  1. The officer opens KYC review (K-W2). The queue shows cases in MANUAL_REVIEW, the oldest first. The tab
      Awaiting second approval shows HIGH cases with one approval.
  2. The officer opens a case (K-W3); KYC assigns it to the officer (assignedOfficerId). The officer reviews the
      snapshot, the checks, the risk breakdown and the documents. Every document view is written to the audit trail.
  3. The officer may clear a HIT as a false positive with a comment; KYC then recalculates the score.
  4. The officer chooses Approve, Request information or Reject (K-BR-08). KYC stores a ReviewDecision and writes
      a KycAuditEvent.
  5. Approve on a LOW or MEDIUM case: APPROVED and KYC_APPROVED. Approve on a HIGH case:
      AWAITING_SECOND_APPROVAL; the case appears in the second tab for all other officers. When a different
      officer approves, the case becomes APPROVED.
  6. Request information: KYC creates an InformationRequest with the requested document types and the due date,
      sets PENDING_CUSTOMER and publishes KYC_INFO_REQUESTED. Reject: REJECTED and KYC_REJECTED with a
      reason code.
Alternative paths
  •     3A Same officer gives the second approval. KYC answers 403 SAME_OFFICER_APPROVAL; in K-W3 the Approve
        button is disabled for that officer.
  •     3B Two officers decide at the same time. Optimistic locking (KycCase.version) accepts the first decision; the
        second officer gets 409 VERSION_CONFLICT and the screen reloads.

6.5.4      Flow 4: Periodic review and change review
  1. The daily job opens a PERIODIC_REVIEW case 30 days before the due date (K-BR-10), or CRM’s
     SENSITIVE_DATA_CHANGED opens a CHANGE_REVIEW case (K-BR-12). KYC publishes KYC_CASE_OPENED (with
     the due date) and KYC_INFO_REQUESTED with the documents the customer must provide, so Notification
     informs the customer.
  2. The customer confirms the data or uploads the requested documents in K-W1 and presses Submit.
  3. KYC re-runs IDV for new identity documents, publishes SCREENING_REQUESTED for all parties again and
     recalculates the score.
  4. If the level is LOW or MEDIUM, is not higher than before and there is no HIT, the case is approved automatically
     and KYC publishes KYC_APPROVED with the new nextReviewDate. Otherwise the case goes to manual review
     (Flow 3).
  5. If the case is not APPROVED 30 days after the due date, it becomes EXPIRED and KYC publishes KYC_EXPIRED;
     CRM restricts the customer (7.5.4). A later KYC_APPROVED lifts the restriction.

approvals. KYC never blocks a customer itself; if an officer decides to block, they do it in Customer 360 (C-W1).

6.7        Non-functional requirements
The shared non-functional requirements (chapter 4) apply. Module-specific targets:
  •    Security and privacy: documents and personal data are AES-256 (Advanced Encryption Standard) encrypted at
       rest; every document view is audited; the four-eyes rule is enforced on the server; only the role COMPLIANCE
       can decide and clear hits; the role ADMIN can read and export the audit trail; a customer sees only their own
       cases (K-BR-14).
  •    Resilience: an IDV, registry or AML outage never stops case intake: retries with backoff, a circuit breaker and
       escalation to MANUAL_REVIEW after 24 hours (alternative 1D).
  •    Performance: API (application programming interface) p95 (the value that 95% of measurements do not exceed)
       below 500 ms (uploads excluded); a 10 MB upload below 5 s; automated checks finished within 60 s for 95 % of
       cases (goal G1); 100 new cases per minute at peak.
  •    Integration and retention: events keyed by customerId, idempotent consumers (processed eventIds are stored),
       transactional outbox, additive schema changes. Evidence, cases and audit events are kept 8 years after the end
       of the customer relationship and then deleted; KycAuditEvent is append-only.

ID             Rule
C-BR-01        Uniqueness. E-mail is unique across users (case-insensitive). Personal code is unique across INDIVIDUAL
               customers. Registration number plus country is unique across LEGAL_ENTITY customers.
C-BR-02        Password. 10–64 characters with at least one letter and one digit, stored as an Argon2id hash. Five wrong

C-BR-04        Customer status. The statuses and the allowed transitions are in 7.5.4. Every transition is written to

7.5        Business flows
7.5.1      Flow 1: Registration and activation of an individual
Actors: customer, CRM, KYC, Notification, other modules. Precondition: the e-mail is not used (C-BR-01).
  1. The customer opens Register on the sign-in screen and fills in name, e-mail, phone, password and language (C-
     W3, step 1). CRM validates the data, creates the User (PENDING) and the Customer (INDIVIDUAL, PROSPECT)
     and returns a registration token valid for 24 hours.
  2. The customer accepts the terms and the DATA_PROCESSING consent and chooses the optional consents (step
     2). CRM stores the consents, sets the user ACTIVE and publishes CUSTOMER_REGISTERED. KYC opens the
     onboarding case; Notification stores the contact details and the language.
  3. The app opens the sign-in screen (S9). The customer signs in with e-mail, password and the SMS (short message
     service) code (CBS F6; CRM checks the password, 7.5.3).
  4. CRM consumes KYC_CASE_OPENED, sets the customer ONBOARDING and the replica IN_PROGRESS.
  5. The customer completes the profile in My profile (C-W2): personal code, date of birth, nationality and address.
     Each save publishes CUSTOMER_PROFILE_UPDATED; KYC stores a new snapshot. The customer then completes
     KYC (K-W1).
  6. CRM consumes KYC_APPROVED, stores the risk level and the next review date, sets the customer ACTIVE and
     publishes CUSTOMER_ACTIVATED.
  7. CBS, Cards and Loans now accept the customer, Accounting and AML register it, and Notification informs the
     customer.

  •     1B Step 2 not completed. After 24 hours the PENDING user and the PROSPECT customer are deleted; nothing
        was published, so no other module knows them.
  •     1C KYC rejected. CRM consumes KYC_REJECTED, sets the replica REJECTED and the customer back to PROSPECT.
        The customer can sign in but cannot open products. A compliance officer may block the customer with a reason.
  •     1D KYC expired during onboarding. CRM consumes KYC_EXPIRED, sets the replica EXPIRED and the customer
        back to PROSPECT. When the customer starts a new case, KYC_CASE_OPENED sets ONBOARDING again.
  •     1E KYC unavailable. CRM keeps working; the KYC status in the app and in Customer 360 shows the last known
        value.

CBS runs the sign-in flow (8.5.6 F6) and the one-time code. CRM only checks the password, because it owns the
password hash. In step 1 of F6 CBS calls POST /internal/crm/users/authenticate with e-mail and password.

  2. CRM compares the password with the hash. A wrong password increases failedLogins; the fifth one in a row sets
     lockedUntil = now + 15 minutes (C-BR-02). The answer for an unknown e-mail and for a wrong password is the
     same: 401 INVALID_CREDENTIALS.

     customer) and customerStatus. CBS continues only when the user is ACTIVE and the customer is not BLOCKED or
     CLOSED; in every other case it shows the neutral message “Sign-in failed.”

7.7          Non-functional requirements
The shared non-functional requirements (chapter 4) apply. Module-specific targets:
  •   Security and privacy: personal code, date of birth, addresses and the old and new values in the history are
      encrypted at field level (AES-256 (Advanced Encryption Standard)); passwords are stored only as Argon2id
      hashes and never published; only COMPLIANCE and ADMIN change a status; every change is written to
      CustomerChangeLog.
  •   Resilience: other modules keep working from their status replicas when CRM is down; CRM keeps working when
      KYC is down (last known KYC value). Sign-in depends on the password check, so CRM is a core service (99.9 %,
      NFR-2) and runs at least two instances.
  •   Performance: password check p95 (the value that 95% of measurements do not exceed) below 200 ms; profile
      read p95 below 300 ms; search p95 below 1 s on 5 million customers; 200 requests per second sustained; a
      status change reaches all consumers within 5 s (p95, goal G3).
  •   Consistency and privacy rights: status change, history entry and event are saved in one transaction (outbox);
      optimistic locking (Customer.version) prevents lost updates; data export within 24 hours; erasure after closure
      and the 8-year retention period.

8        CBS and Payment system
8.1      Scope and ownership
The Core Banking System (CBS) is the system of record for accounts, balances, balance holds and account transactions.
The Payment system turns a payment instruction (internal, SEPA (Single Euro Payments Area) or SWIFT (Society for
Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication)) into a transaction: it calculates fee and FX (foreign exchange),
asks for strong customer authentication (SCA), asks AML (anti-money laundering) to screen the payment, handles
dual-signature approval and settles through the payment networks. CBS also contains the identity and access function
(the sign-in flow, sessions and one-time codes, called IAM (identity and access management) in the CRM (customer
relationship management) chapter), because it protects every payment.

 BR-01        IBAN (International Bank Account Number): CBS (core banking system) generates the bank's own IBANs as LT
              (country code of Lithuania) + 18 digits (20 characters) with valid mod-97 (modulo 97, the standard bank-account
              check calculation) check digits, unique. A beneficiary IBAN can belong to another country: it must start with a
              known country code, have the length of that country (for example LT 20, DE 22 characters, never more than 34)
              and have valid mod-97 check digits.
 BR-02        Available balance = balance - sum(ACTIVE holds). A payment needs availableBalance >= amount + fee.
 BR-03        Daily limit: default EUR (euro) 10,000 outgoing per account per calendar day (Europe/Vilnius), kept in
              AccountSettings.dailyLimit. Completed payments and ACTIVE holds count.
 BR-04        SCA (strong customer authentication): a 6-digit code is required for login, for every payment above EUR 500 (EUR
              equivalent), for every payment approval, for every mandate change, and for card activation and credit-card
              repayment (chapter 9). CBS generates the code and publishes OTP_REQUESTED; Notification delivers it by SMS
              (short message service). A code is valid 5 minutes and allows 3 attempts. SCA is done once per payment, by the
              person who authorizes it (the customer, or the Director in a dual-signature flow).
 BR-05        Fees (in EUR, debited in the account currency at the quoted rate): internal EUR 0.00, SEPA (Single Euro Payments
              Area) EUR 0.20, SWIFT (Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication) EUR 5.00.
 BR-06        Currency: SEPA payments are in EUR only. If the payment currency differs from the account currency, CBS
              converts at the quoted bank rate plus a 1.0% markup. The quote is fixed when the user confirms. Exchange rates
              are kept in an FxRate table with the currency pair, the rate and validFrom. In this project they are loaded from the
              default data and updated once a day by a mock rate provider.
 BR-07        Dual signature: if AccountSettings.dualSignatureRequired is true, every outgoing payment needs approval by a
              user with APPROVE_PAYMENTS who is not the creator. A pending payment expires after 7 days.


                                              E-Bank technical specification | page 33
 BR-08          Status effects: account FROZEN blocks outgoing payments, incoming funds are still accepted; CLOSED rejects
                everything. Customer status RESTRICTED blocks outgoing payments and new accounts, incoming funds are
                accepted; BLOCKED blocks login and all operations.
 BR-09          Closing an account needs balance 0.00 and no ACTIVE hold.
 BR-10          Validation: amount above 0 with at most 2 decimals; beneficiary name 1-70 and purpose 1-140 characters; BIC
                (Bank Identifier Code) 8 or 11 characters, required for SWIFT.
 BR-11          Transaction records: Transaction.amount is negative for a debit and positive for a credit. An internal transfer is
                one transaction on the source account with toAccountId set; the recipient’s history shows it as a credit.
                Counterparty country is taken from the IBAN prefix (BIC characters 5-6 if there is no IBAN).
 BR-12          Idempotency: every POST that creates a payment or posting carries an Idempotency-Key header; a repeated key
                returns the first result. The key is stored in Payment.idempotencyKey (payments) and
                Transaction.idempotencyKey (postings from Cards and Loans).
 BR-13          Holds: ACTIVE while a payment is processed, CONSUMED when the money is deducted, RELEASED on failure,
                rejection, cancellation or expiry. The balance is deducted only when a hold is consumed.
 BR-14          AML (anti-money laundering) contract: outgoing payments are screened before execution (synchronous call, 2 s
                timeout). If AML does not answer within 2 s or returns a 5xx error and the amount is above EUR 1,000, the
                transaction stays ON_HOLD and the call is repeated every minute, at most 5 times; after the fifth failure CBS
                publishes AML_SCREENING_FAILED and the transaction stays ON_HOLD until the AML officer decides. Up to EUR
                1,000 the payment is executed and AML checks it afterwards. The screening call in section 11.8.1 also sends
                referenceId, initiatedByUserId and amountEur. referenceId is the Transaction id. initiatedByUserId is the user who
                created the payment. amountEur is the amount converted to EUR with the CBS exchange rate at that moment,
                without the FX markup. The 1,000 EUR limit compares amountEur. A repeated call uses the same referenceId, so
                AML returns the first decision.
 BR-15          Sessions: after login CBS returns an access token valid 5 minutes and a refresh token. The app renews the access
                token with the refresh token; CBS refuses this when the last activity of the Session was more than 15 minutes ago,
                so a session ends after 15 minutes of inactivity (NFR-1). Logout ends the session at once.

8.5.1      F1: Account opening and mandate assignment
 •      Actors: Customer, Legal entity admin, CBS (Account Core Service), SCA (strong customer authentication) service.
 •      Preconditions: customer status ACTIVE (not RESTRICTED) and kycStatus = APPROVED.
 •      Main scenario:
          1. The customer requests an account with type (CURRENT, SAVINGS) and currency (EUR, USD, GBP).
          2. CBS creates the Account (OPEN) with a unique IBAN (International Bank Account Number) and
              AccountSettings with the default limit, and publishes ACCOUNT_OPENED. For individuals the flow ends
              here.
          3. Legal entities: the admin opens Manage mandates, selects a user, role and permissions (VIEW,
              CREATE_PAYMENTS, APPROVE_PAYMENTS) and may switch on dual signature.
          4. The admin enters a one-time code (BR-04). CBS saves the AccountMandate records and publishes
              MANDATE_CHANGED.
 •      Alternative 1A, invalid user: the user is not ACTIVE or not KYC-verified. CBS answers “Assigned user must have an
        active and verified KYC (know your customer) status.” and the admin returns to step 3.
 •      Alternative 1B, code wrong or expired: nothing is saved.
 •      Alternative 1C, last admin or approver: a change is rejected if it leaves the account without an ADMIN, or with
        dual signature on and nobody holding APPROVE_PAYMENTS.
 •      Alternative 1D, customer restricted: the request in step 1 is rejected with “Your profile is restricted. Contact
        support.”

8.5.2      F2: Transfer execution (internal, SEPA, SWIFT)
 •      Actors: User (customer, or Director in a dual-signature flow), Payment engine, Hold service, SCA service, AML
        (anti-money laundering), Payment gateway, Event bus.
 •      Preconditions: source account OPEN, customer not RESTRICTED, user has CREATE_PAYMENTS (or owns the
        account). A payment coming from F3 is already APPROVED, its hold exists and SCA is done.
 •      Main scenario:
          1. The user enters source account, recipient (IBAN, BIC (Bank Identifier Code) for SWIFT (Society for
              Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication)), amount, currency and purpose. CBS creates a
              Payment (DRAFT) and shows fee and FX (foreign exchange) rate (PAY-06), calculated from the latest
              FxRate (BR-06).
          2. The user confirms. CBS checks account status, customer status, daily limit and available balance, then
              creates an AccountHold (ACTIVE) for amount plus fee.
          3. If the amount is above EUR (euro) 500 the user enters a one-time code.
          4. CBS creates the Transaction (PENDING) and calls AML with the request of rule BR-14. AML answers
              ALLOW.
          5. CBS routes the payment. Internal: moves the money to the target account. SEPA/SWIFT: sends the
              message to the payment gateway and waits for confirmation.
          6. On settlement CBS deducts the balance, sets the hold CONSUMED, Transaction = COMPLETED, Payment =
              EXECUTED, and publishes TRANSACTION_COMPLETED (one event per notified party for internal transfers).
 •      Alternative 2A, insufficient funds or limit exceeded (step 2): no hold is created, Payment = FAILED, message
        “Insufficient available balance or daily limit exceeded.”
 •      Alternative 2B, code wrong 3 times or expired (step 3): hold RELEASED, Payment = FAILED,
        TRANSACTION_FAILED (reason SCA_FAILED).
 •      Alternative 2C, AML answers HOLD (step 4): Transaction = ON_HOLD, the hold stays ACTIVE. When an AML
        officer decides, AML publishes TRANSACTION_RELEASED (processing continues at step 5) or
        TRANSACTION_REJECTED (Transaction = REJECTED, Payment = FAILED, hold RELEASED). CBS acts on these
        events only while the transaction is ON_HOLD and ignores them otherwise. The customer is informed by AML's
        neutral message only; CBS publishes no other event, so the reason is never shown. The 60 s limit of 2E does not
        apply while the transaction is ON_HOLD.
 •      Alternative 2D, AML does not answer within 2 s or returns a 5xx error (step 4): rule BR-14: if amountEur is above
        EUR 1,000 the transaction is held and the call is repeated with the same referenceId; up to EUR 1,000 processing
        continues at step 5.

8.5.5      F5: Account administration (employee)
A short action-response flow, described without a diagram. Actors: employee with role SUPPORT or ADMIN. Steps: (1)
the employee finds the customer in CRM (customer relationship management) and opens the account on screen W-
CBS-10; (2) chooses Freeze, Unfreeze, Close or a new daily limit and enters a mandatory reason; (3) CBS validates the
change, updates the account, writes an AuditLogEntry to the shared audit log (section 8.8) and publishes
ACCOUNT_STATUS_CHANGED for status changes. Alternatives: 5A closing is rejected while the balance is not 0.00 or a
hold is ACTIVE (“Account cannot be closed: balance must be zero and no active holds.”); 5B an invalid status change
(for example freezing a CLOSED account) is rejected; 5C a daily limit above EUR 100,000 needs the role ADMIN.

8.5.6      F6: Login and strong customer authentication
  •     Actors: User (customer or bank employee), CBS (SCA service), Notification (delivers the SMS (short message
        service)).
  •     Preconditions: the user is registered in CRM (customer relationship management), which owns the password
        hash. Employee users are loaded from the default data of CRM.
  •     Main scenario:
          1. The user enters e-mail and password. CBS calls POST /internal/crm/users/authenticate (section 7.5.3);
               CRM checks the password and returns the user and customer status. CBS continues only if the user is
               ACTIVE and the customer is not BLOCKED or CLOSED. A customer who is still onboarding (no
               CustomerReplica yet) can log in, so that the KYC (know your customer) documents can be uploaded; until
               CUSTOMER_ACTIVATED, CBS account and payment endpoints answer CUSTOMER_NOT_ACTIVE.
          2. CBS creates an OtpChallenge (purpose LOGIN) and publishes OTP_REQUESTED (userId, code, purpose);
               Notification sends the code by SMS.
          3. The user enters the code. CBS checks it, creates a Session and returns an access token and a refresh token
               (rule BR-15: the session ends after 15 minutes of inactivity). CBS updates UserDevice; a device not seen
               before also publishes NEW_DEVICE_LOGIN (userId, deviceName, city).
  •     Alternative 6A, wrong code: the attempt is counted; after 3 attempts the challenge is FAILED and the user starts
        again.
  •     Alternative 6B, expired code: after 5 minutes the user requests a new code (at most 3 per 15 minutes).
  •     Alternative 6C, wrong password, locked user or blocked customer: the same neutral message “Sign-in failed.” is
        shown.
  •     Operations: payments, approvals, mandate changes, card activation and credit-card repayment use the same
        challenge with another purpose and referenceId; a successful check returns a single-use scaToken bound to that
        purpose and reference, valid for 5 minutes. Cards checks the token with POST /internal/cbs/sca/validate.
  •     Employees log in to the back-office with the same flow. CBS keeps a read-only copy of the Employee list
        (EmployeeReplica), loaded from the same default data as AML (anti-money laundering), and puts the employee
        role into the token at login.

8.8          Data model
Account and Transaction are shared entities; other modules reference them by id. CBS (core banking system) added
the nullable fields toAccountId, paymentId and referenceId, the counterparty fields, completedAt, the type value
CARD_REPAYMENT and the status ON_HOLD to Transaction, which AML (anti-money laundering), Accounting, Cards
and Notification need. paymentId is empty for postings made by Cards and Loans, which have no Payment; their
referenceId holds the card authorization or loan id. idempotencyKey (rule BR-12) is stored on Payment and on
Transaction, so a repeated request can be recognised. Customer and User belong to other modules, and
AuditLogEntry to the shared audit log (AML chapter, section 11.5). CBS owns the login data (Session, UserDevice,
OtpChallenge; the password hash belongs to CRM), keeps EmployeeReplica, a read-only copy of the Employee list, and
FxRate, the exchange rates (BR-06). The diagram is split into two parts.

                EXPIRED the next day. A replacement gets a new number, last4 and expiry.
CD-BR-04        Spending limits: a new card has a per-purchase limit of 500 EUR and a daily limit of 1,500 EUR. The customer can

9.3        Business flows
9.3.1      Order and activate
  •  Actors: Customer, Cards, CBS (accounts and one-time codes), Notification (delivers the code).
  •  Preconditions: the customer is signed in and ACTIVE. For a debit card, the customer has an open EUR account.
     For a credit card, the order follows section 9.3.5.
Main scenario:
  1. The customer opens CD3, chooses Debit and an own open EUR account, and orders the card.
  2. Cards reads the customer status from its replica and checks the account with GET /internal/cbs/accounts/{id}:
     owner, OPEN and EUR.
  3. Cards creates the CardOrder (ISSUED), the Card (INACTIVE) with its number and expiry (CD-BR-02, CD-BR-03)
     and the CardLimit with the default limits (CD-BR-04).
  4. The app asks CBS for a code with purpose CARD_ACTIVATION and the card id as the reference. CBS publishes
     OTP_REQUESTED, and Notification sends the code by SMS (short message service).
  5. The customer enters the code. CBS checks it and returns a scaToken (SCA: strong customer authentication).
  6. The app sends the activation with the token. Cards validates it with CBS and sets the card to ACTIVE.
  • Alternative 1A, customer or account not eligible: Cards answers CUSTOMER_NOT_ELIGIBLE or
     ACCOUNT_NOT_OPEN and creates no card.
  • Alternative 1B, wrong or expired code: the card stays INACTIVE. After 3 wrong codes the customer requests a
     new one.
  • Alternative 1C, no customer replica yet: Cards reads the status with GET /internal/crm/customers/{id} (section
     7.10.3).

9.3.2    Block or replace
  •  Actors: Customer, Cards, Notification.
  •  Preconditions: the card belongs to the customer. A temporary block needs an ACTIVE card; unblock needs a
     TEMP_BLOCKED card.
Main scenario:
  1. The customer chooses Block card on CD2. Cards sets the status to TEMP_BLOCKED. New authorizations are
     declined at once, and CARD_BLOCKED is published.
  2. The customer chooses Unblock. If the customer is still eligible (and, for debit, the linked account is open), the
     card becomes ACTIVE.
  3. The customer chooses Report lost or stolen and confirms. The status becomes PERM_BLOCKED, which cannot
     be undone, and CARD_BLOCKED is published.
  4. The customer orders a replacement. Cards creates a new INACTIVE card with replacesCardId set, linked to the
     same account or the same CardCredit. A credit-card replacement keeps the same credit limit and outstanding
     balance. Activation continues as in section 9.3.1 from step 4.
  • Alternative 2A, repeated block: the status does not change and no new event is published.
  • Alternative 2B, not eligible at unblock: Cards answers CUSTOMER_NOT_ELIGIBLE or ACCOUNT_NOT_OPEN, and
     the card stays TEMP_BLOCKED.

9.3.3    Authorize a purchase
  •  Actors: Card network, Cards, AML, CBS (debit cards).
  •  Preconditions: the network sends a unique networkRequestId with the card number, amount, currency and
     merchant.
Main scenario:
  1. Cards returns the saved answer if this networkRequestId was already processed.
  2. Cards finds the card and checks card and customer status, the EUR currency and the spending limits. For a debit
     card it also checks the account with GET /internal/cbs/accounts/{id}.
  3. Cards first saves the CardAuthorization (PENDING), then calls AML as described in section 11.8.1, with source
     CARD, referenceId set to the CardAuthorization id, initiatedByUserId set to the card holder, and amountEur
     (purchases are in EUR, so it equals the amount). accountId is null for a credit card.
  4. Cards reserves the money. For a debit card it calls POST /internal/cbs/holds with referenceType
     CARD_AUTHORIZATION and expiresAt 7 days from now, and stores holdId and transactionId. For a credit card it
     adds the amount to pendingAmount if the available credit is enough.
  5. Cards saves APPROVED, answers the network and publishes CARD_TRANSACTION_AUTHORIZED.
  6. The card network reports completion. For a debit card, Cards calls capture on the hold, and CBS sets the
     transaction to COMPLETED. For a credit card, the amount moves from pendingAmount to outstandingAmount.
     The status becomes COMPLETED.
  • Alternative 3A, a check in step 2 fails: the attempt is DECLINED with a reason code, and AML is not called.
  • Alternative 3B, AML returns HOLD: Cards declines this attempt; the card is not blocked. A later AML decision
     does not charge the customer.
  • Alternative 3C, AML does not answer: if AML does not answer within 2 seconds or returns a 5xx error, Cards
     does not retry: above 1,000 EUR it declines the attempt, and at 1,000 EUR or less it approves it. AML then
     checks it later from CARD_TRANSACTION_AUTHORIZED.


                                          E-Bank technical specification | page 54
•   Alternative 3D, funds cannot be reserved: CBS answers INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS, DAILY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED or
    ACCOUNT_NOT_OPEN, or does not answer, or the available credit is too low. The attempt is DECLINED.
•   Alternative 3E, cancellation: the network reports a cancellation. Cards calls cancel on the hold, or releases
    pendingAmount for a credit card. The status becomes CANCELLED.
•   Alternative 3F, no report within 7 days: CBS releases the hold itself (section 8.5.4, 4B). The Scheduler sets the

9.3.4    Repay used credit
 • Actors: Customer, Cards, CBS.
 • Preconditions: the credit card has an outstanding amount, and the customer has an own open EUR account.
Main scenario:
  1. The customer opens CD4, enters the amount and chooses the account. Cards checks that the amount is not

  2. The customer confirms with a one-time code (purpose CARD_REPAYMENT, reference the repayment id) and the

  3. Cards calls POST /internal/cbs/postings/debit with type CARD_REPAYMENT, and the repayment id as

      outstanding amount does not change.
  • Alternative 4C, CBS does not answer: Cards retries with the same Idempotency-Key and backoff (section 3.2). If
      it still fails, the repayment is FAILED.

10.4       Business flows
10.4.1     Loan application
A customer with approved KYC submits a loan application. The system validates it and calculates a credit score. [1] A
low score (below 400) is rejected automatically, and a high score (above 700) is approved automatically. A score in
between goes to a loan officer, who approves it or rejects it with a reason. Once approved, the customer reviews the
offer and either signs or declines it. After signing, the system asks CBS (core banking system) to pay out the money,
and the installment schedule is created.
Credit score calculation
The score is a number from 300 to 850 calculated from the application data and the customer’s existing loans:
1. New payment estimate = amount / termMonths (interest is ignored in the score).
2. Existing payments = the sum of the monthly installments of the customer’s other loans with status DISBURSED (0 if
the customer has none). Loans reads them from its own data.
3. Payment-to-income ratio = (new payment estimate + existing payments) / monthly income.
4. Score = 800 − round(1000 × ratio), limited to the range 300–850.
Complete formula, in one line:
score = 800 − round(1000 × ((amount / termMonths + existingPayments) / monthlyIncome)), and the result is never
below 300 or above 850.
Where: amount and termMonths come from the application, monthlyIncome is the income stated in the application,
and existingPayments is the sum of the monthly installments of the customer’s other DISBURSED loans (0 if none).
Example: monthlyIncome 3,000, amount 6,000, termMonths 24, existingPayments 300. New payment = 6,000 / 24 =
250. Ratio = (250 + 300) / 3,000 = 0.183. Score = 800 − round(1000 × 0.183) = 800 − 183 = 617. This is between 400 and
700, so the application goes to a loan officer.
The lower the share of income that goes to loan payments, the higher the score. An application with income 0 is
rejected at once. The decision thresholds are the ones above: below 400 rejected, above 700 approved, 400–700 goes
to a loan officer. The calculated score and its inputs (income, new payment, existing payments) are stored with the
decision (see LA-NFR-3).

10.4.2   End-of-day posting and reconciliation
Every night at 00:05 (Europe/Vilnius) the batch job collects the completed transactions of the previous calendar day
from CBS and the completed credit-card purchases of the same day from Cards, and posts each one to the ledger as a
debit entry and a credit entry, using the posting rules. Transactions without a rule are skipped and flagged. The system


                                           E-Bank technical specification | page 68
then compares total debits with total credits. If they match, the run is complete. If not, the discrepancy is saved with
the run date, shown to employees with the role ACCOUNTANT in General ledger and reports, and written to the audit
log. The accountant investigates and resolves it there. No customer notification is sent. The day is chosen by the time
a transaction was completed (from 00:00:00 up to, but not including, 00:00:00 of the next day), not by the time the
batch starts, so every transaction belongs to exactly one day, including those completed between 23:59 and 00:00.
The batch calls GET /internal/cbs/transactions with from and to set to the Europe/Vilnius midnights converted to UTC
(Coordinated Universal Time); CBS compares them with Transaction.completedAt.




                                            End-of-day posting and reconciliation

Posting rules map the CBS transaction types (see the CBS chapter) to ledger entries. Transactions without a rule are
skipped and flagged. The CBS type CARD_REPAYMENT (credit-card repayment from a bank account) is posted with the
rule CardRepayment: debit customer account liability, credit card receivable. A completed credit-card purchase has no
CBS transaction; the batch reads it from Cards (section 9.9.3) and posts it with the rule CardCreditPurchase: debit card
receivable, credit card network settlement. Its LedgerEntry.transactionId holds the card authorization id. A loan
installment (LoanInstallment) is posted as one amount: CBS records only the total amount of the debit, so Accounting
does not split it into principal and interest. Every posting rule therefore has one debit ledger account and one credit
ledger account for the full amount.

11        AML
11.1      Scope and ownership
The AML (Anti-Money Laundering) system checks customer transactions for signs of money laundering and terrorist
financing. It helps compliance officers investigate suspicious cases and report them to the Financial Crime
Investigation Service (FNTT), Lithuania's financial intelligence unit (FIU). Banks are required by law to do this.
AML connects to the rest of the bank in two ways. Before a transaction is executed, the Payment system and Card
management ask AML for a decision and wait for the answer. After a transaction is completed, AML receives the
transaction event and looks for patterns across several transactions.
Module boundaries:
  •    Sanctions and PEP (politically exposed person) screening during onboarding is requested by KYC
       (SCREENING_REQUESTED) and answered by AML (SCREENING_COMPLETED, CLEAR or HIT). AML matches the
       person or company against its watchlist (section 11.7); KYC keeps the decision and the customer risk level. AML
       uses the risk level in its transaction score.
  •    AML does not read other modules' databases. It keeps its own copies of the transaction and customer data it
       needs (AmlTransaction and AmlCustomer, section 11.7), filled only from screening requests and events.
  •    AML does not send messages to customers itself. It publishes events, and the Notification system sends the
       messages.
  •    FNTT is an external system. It is described as an interface in section 11.8.4, so it can be replaced with a mock
       during implementation.

11.3     Business flows
11.3.1   F1: Screen a transaction
Actors: Payment system, Card management, AML system.
Preconditions: at least one active AmlRule exists, and the Customer has a KYC risk level (LOW, MEDIUM or HIGH) in
AmlCustomer.
Basic course:
  1. The Payment system creates a Transaction with status PENDING and sends AML a screening request (section
     11.8.1).
  2. The AML system saves the transaction in AmlTransaction and loads the Customer's risk level and all active
     AmlRules.
  3. The AML system checks the transaction against each active AmlRule. The default rules are:
       ○ R1, large amount: amountEur is more than 15,000 EUR (euro).
       ○ R2, structuring: the current transfer and the Customer's outgoing transfers completed in the 24 hours
           before it (a rolling window over all accounts of the Customer) are counted together. R2 matches when
           there are 5 or more such transfers, each below 15,000 EUR, and their total is above 15,000 EUR.




                                               E-Bank technical specification | page 76
        ○ R3, high-risk country: the counterparty's country (for cards, the merchant's country) is in the
           HighRiskCountry table. The starting list contains KP (North Korea) and IR (Iran), as ISO 3166-1 alpha-2
           codes.
  4. The AML system finds no matching rule and returns ALLOW.
  5. The Payment system executes the transaction.
Amounts: all thresholds are in EUR. The caller sends amountEur: the amount converted with the CBS (core banking
system) exchange rate at the time of the transaction, without the FX (foreign exchange) markup. AML compares only
amountEur with the thresholds.
Risk score: the sum of the weights of the matched rules, multiplied by the customer risk factor (LOW 1.0, MEDIUM
1.5, HIGH 2.0), with a maximum of 100. Example: R3 (weight 70) for a MEDIUM customer gives 70 × 1.5 = 105, so the
score is 100. The weights and factors are in section 11.7.
Alternate courses:
  •   A1. A rule matches, but the risk score is below 70 and no matched rule has the action HOLD. The AML system
      creates an AmlAlert with held = false and returns ALLOW. The Payment system executes the transaction. The
      alert appears in the AML task queue to be reviewed afterwards. Alerts with held = false do not stop new
      transactions.
  •   A2. The risk score is 70 or more, or a matched rule has the action HOLD. The AML system creates an AmlAlert
      with held = true and returns HOLD. The Payment system sets the transaction status to ON_HOLD. The
      transaction stays ON_HOLD, with its hold active, until the AML officer decides in F2; the 60-second limit of NFR-2
      does not apply to it.
  •   A3. The Customer already has an open AmlCase, or an AmlAlert with status NEW and held = true. The AML
      system returns HOLD, whatever the risk score, and creates an AmlAlert with held = true. If a case is open, the
      new alert is linked to it; otherwise it is linked when an officer takes the Customer's first alert (F2, step 3).
  •   A4. The AML system does not answer within 2 seconds, or returns a 5xx error. If amountEur is above 1,000 EUR,
      the Payment system sets the transaction to ON_HOLD and repeats the request every minute, up to 5 times (BR-
      14). A repeat carries the same referenceId, so it never creates a second alert. After the fifth failure, the Payment
      system publishes AML_SCREENING_FAILED, and the AML system creates an AmlAlert for manual review (reason
      SCREENING_FAILED, no rule codes, risk score 100, held = true). If amountEur is 1,000 EUR or less, the Payment
      system executes the transaction, and AML checks it later as in A5.
  •   A5. The Payment system publishes TRANSACTION_COMPLETED. The AML system updates the transaction in
      AmlTransaction, or creates it if the transaction was executed without screening. A transaction that was not
      screened before execution (A4 with 1,000 EUR or less, and A6) is checked against all active rules, as in step 3. If a
      rule matches, the AML system creates an AmlAlert with held = false; nothing is held.
  •   A6. Card transaction. Card management calls AML before it reserves funds, with source = CARD and referenceId
      = the CardAuthorization id, and the AML system screens it as in steps 2 and 3. HOLD means that Cards declines
      this attempt; the card itself is not blocked (section 9.3.3). The AmlAlert is created with held = false, because no
      transaction waits for a decision, and AML never publishes TRANSACTION_RELEASED or TRANSACTION_REJECTED
      for card alerts. If AML does not answer within 2 seconds or returns a 5xx error, Cards does not retry: above
      1,000 EUR it declines the attempt; at 1,000 EUR or less it approves it, and AML checks it afterwards from
      CARD_TRANSACTION_AUTHORIZED (A5).
  •   A7. The Customer was rejected by KYC (kycStatus = REJECTED in AmlCustomer). The AML system returns HOLD
      and creates an AmlAlert with held = true. Normally this does not happen, because such a customer never
      becomes ACTIVE and CBS does not accept their payments.

11.3.2    F2: Investigate a suspicious case
Actors: AML officer (Employee with the role COMPLIANCE), AML system, Payment system, Notification system, FNTT.
Preconditions: an AmlAlert with status NEW exists, and the AML officer is logged in to the back-office.
Basic course (the activity turns out to be legitimate):
  0. The AML officer clicks AML queue in the back-office menu. The system shows the AML task queue (W-AML-01),
     sorted by risk score.
  1. The AML officer clicks Take on an AmlAlert.
  2. The system creates an AmlCase with status IN_REVIEW and assigns it to the AML officer. It links this alert and all
     other NEW alerts of the same Customer to the case and sets their status to IN_CASE. It opens Case details (W-
     AML-02).



                                             E-Bank technical specification | page 78
  3. The AML officer reviews the triggered rules, the transaction, the Customer panel, the 90-day history and the
      earlier cases.
  4. The AML officer types a comment, clicks Release and confirms.
  5. The system closes the AmlCase as CLOSED_FALSE_POSITIVE and sets its alerts to CLOSED. For every alert of the
      case with held = true, it publishes TRANSACTION_RELEASED. It returns the AML officer to the AML task queue.
  6. The Payment system executes the released transactions.
Every action in this flow is written to the audit log (AuditEntry, section 11.7), with the values before and after.
Alternate courses:
  ● A1. The suspicion is confirmed. At step 5, the AML officer types a comment and clicks Report to FNTT. In the
    dialog, the officer types the reason, selects the related transactions from the Customer's transactions of the last
    90 days and clicks Submit. The system sends the report to FNTT (section 11.8.4), saves the returned reference in
    FiuReport, closes the AmlCase as CLOSED_REPORTED and sets its alerts to CLOSED. For every alert with held =
    true, it publishes TRANSACTION_REJECTED. The Payment system sets those transactions to REJECTED, and the
    Customer receives the message "Your payment could not be executed. Please contact the bank." Nothing the
    Customer can see mentions the report, because AML law prohibits this (tipping-off).
  ● A2. FNTT does not answer within 10 seconds or returns an error. At A1, the system shows "FNTT is unavailable.
    The report was not sent. Please try again." The AmlCase stays IN_REVIEW, and the held transactions stay
    ON_HOLD.
  ● A3. Another officer took the alert first. At step 2, the system shows "This alert has already been taken" and
    reloads the AML task queue.
  ● A4. No alert of the case has held = true: its transactions were already executed (F1, A1 and A5) or declined (F1,
    A6). At step 5 the button is labelled Close as false positive. The system closes the case as in step 6 but publishes
    no event. If the officer reports the case instead (A1), the report is sent and the case is closed as
    CLOSED_REPORTED, but no TRANSACTION_REJECTED is published. CBS ignores TRANSACTION_RELEASED and
    TRANSACTION_REJECTED for a transaction that is not ON_HOLD.
  ● A5. The alert was created from AML_SCREENING_FAILED (F1, A4). The triggered rules panel shows "Screening
    failed". The officer decides as in the basic course or in A1.

12.1      Scope and ownership
The Notification system is a shared service used by every module. It receives events from the other modules, turns
them into messages from templates, and sends them to customers by push, e-mail or SMS (short message service).
Customers choose on which channels they receive each category of message. Mandatory messages, including all
security messages, are always sent.
Module boundaries:
  •    The Notification system never decides on its own what to send or to whom. It only reacts to events. If a module
       needs a new message, it publishes a new event, and a template for that event is added here.
  •    Every event that has a template contains userId: the user to notify, chosen by the module that publishes the
       event (as in the CBS (core banking system) events, section 8.10.3). If several users must be notified (for example
       the approvers of a company payment), the module publishes one event per user.
  •    The Notification system sends messages only to customers. Messages for employees (for example a
       reconciliation discrepancy) are shown in the back-office of the module that owns them.
  •    One-time confirmation codes are generated by CBS. The Notification system only delivers them.
  •    The content of a message comes from the event payload. The Notification system does not read other modules'
       databases: it keeps its own copy of the contact details it needs (RecipientContact, section 12.7), filled from CRM
       events, and its own push tokens and language settings.
  •    The push service, e-mail provider and SMS provider are external systems. They are described as one interface in
       section 12.8.2, so they can be replaced with mocks during implementation.

12.3      Business flows
12.3.1    F1: Send a notification
Actors: the module that publishes the event, the Notification system, the channel provider, the Customer.

                                                 E-Bank technical specification | page 87
Preconditions: a module has published an event that has a template in section 12.8.4, with the userId of the user to
notify.
Basic course (a notification that is not mandatory):
  •   The Notification system receives the event, for example TRANSACTION_COMPLETED.
  •   It reads userId from the payload and loads the user's notification language (LT or EN) from NotificationSettings.
  •   It loads the NotificationTemplate for the event type, variant and language, and fills in the placeholders from the
      payload.
  • It loads the user's NotificationPreference for the template's category and takes every enabled channel. A missing
      preference row means the defaults in section 12.7.1.
  • For each enabled channel, it saves a Notification with status PENDING and sends the message to that channel's
      provider: to every PushToken of the user, or to the e-mail address or phone number in RecipientContact.
  • The provider returns DELIVERED. The Notification system sets that Notification to DELIVERED and records
      sentAt. A push message counts as delivered when at least one device accepted it.
  • The Customer receives the message.
Alternate courses:
  •   A1. The template is mandatory. At step 4, the Notification system ignores the NotificationPreference and sends
      on one channel at a time: push first; if that fails, SMS; then e-mail. Each attempt is its own Notification row, with
      at most 3 attempts in total (NTF-05). OTP_REQUESTED is sent only by SMS, once; if that fails, the customer uses
      Resend in the one-time code dialog (section 5.2), which creates a new code and a new event.
  •   A2. Delivery fails: the provider returns FAILED, does not answer within 30 seconds, or the channel has no
      address (no push token, e-mail or phone). The Notification becomes FAILED, with lastError. A notification that is
      not mandatory is not retried and not moved to another channel; a mandatory one continues as in A1. When no
      channel delivered the message, the system publishes NOTIFICATION_FAILED.
  •   A3. The Customer turned off every channel for this category. The Notification system sends nothing and saves
      no Notification.
  •   A4. No NotificationTemplate exists in the user's language. The Notification system uses the English template.
  •   A5. The same event arrives again (the same eventId). A Notification with that eventId, userId and channel
      already exists, so nothing is sent again (NTF-NFR-02).

12.3.2    F2: Register a push token
Actors: Customer (mobile app), Notification system, push service (Apple or Google).
Preconditions: the customer has logged in on the mobile app.
Basic course:
  1. After login, the mobile app gets a push token for the device from the push service.
  2. The app calls PUT /api/v1/push-tokens/{deviceId} with the platform and the token. deviceId is an id the app
      creates when it is installed.
  3. The Notification system saves the PushToken, or replaces the token of that device.
Alternate courses:
  •    A1. The customer signs out. The app calls DELETE /api/v1/push-tokens/{deviceId}, and the Notification system
       deletes the token.
  •    A2. The push service answers that a token is no longer valid. The Notification system deletes that PushToken.

IBAN                International bank account number. Lithuanian IBANs are LT plus 18 digits.
Idempotency-Key     HTTP header that makes a repeated request return the first result instead of creating a second one.
IDV                 Identity verification provider (document authenticity and liveness check).
KYC                 Know your customer: the process that decides whether the bank may trust a customer’s identity.
Ledger              The general ledger of the bank: balanced debit and credit entries produced by Accounting.
Mandate             The permissions (VIEW, CREATE_PAYMENTS, APPROVE_PAYMENTS) and role a user has on a company
                    account.
Mandatory           A notification sent whatever the customer's channel settings: every security message and the templates
notification        marked mandatory.
MoSCoW              Priority scale: Must, Should, Could, Won't. Won't means deliberately excluded from this release.
Outbox              Pattern in which an event is saved with the business change in one database transaction and published
                    afterwards, so no event is lost.
OTP                 One-time password: the six-digit code used for strong customer authentication.
p95                 The value that 95% of measurements do not exceed (for example response time).
PEP                 Politically exposed person, who needs enhanced checks.
PSD2                EU payment services directive that requires strong customer authentication.
Push token          An identifier from Apple or Google that lets the bank send push messages to one device.
Refresh token       A token the app uses to get a new access token while the session is still active (15 minutes of inactivity at
                    most).
Risk score          The 0 to 100 score of a transaction in AML: the sum of the weights of the matched rules, multiplied by the
                    customer risk factor.
RPO / RTO           Recovery point objective (data that may be lost) and recovery time objective (time to recover).
Sanctions list      A list of persons and companies the bank may not do business with.

SCA           Strong customer authentication: the one-time code at login and for payments above EUR 500.
SEPA          Single Euro Payments Area: euro transfers inside Europe.
Session       The server-side record of a login, ended by logout or by 15 minutes of inactivity.
Structuring   Splitting a large amount into several smaller transfers to stay below a reporting threshold. AML rule R2
              looks for it.
SWIFT         International payment network for transfers that are not SEPA.
Tipping-off   Telling a customer that a suspicious-activity report was made. It is forbidden by AML law and never shown
              in the system.
UBO           Ultimate beneficial owner: a person who owns more than 25% of a company.
Watchlist     The sanctions and PEP entries that AML matches persons and companies against.
